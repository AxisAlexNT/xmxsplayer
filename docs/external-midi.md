# External MIDI instruments

An external instrument sends MIDI to another application or a hardware synthesizer. Its audio does not pass through the app's Main/Cue buses or limiter; configure and monitor the receiving synth separately.

1. Start the receiving software or connect the hardware MIDI interface. In the track's instrument menu choose **Change preset… → External MIDI port…**.
2. Choose its **MIDI output** and **Channel**, then enter a name, bank and program. Channels are displayed as 1–16; YAML stores 0–15. Bank is a 14-bit number: bank MSB × 128 + bank LSB. Programs are entered as 0–127; subtract one if the receiver's manual numbers them 1–128. The app sends CC0, CC32, then program change. Select the bank/program supported by the receiver; no General MIDI reset or SysEx is sent by this backend.
3. Use **Audition**, or play the controller while the picker is open. **Use preset** saves the destination. An external instrument needs no project soundfont. Route any live or sequenced track to it through Patch as usual.
4. Give overlapping sounds separate physical channels. Routes sharing a port/channel share its program, volume, pan, effects, bend and pressure. Duplicate pitches retain their owners until the final release, and sustain remains down while any owner holds it. A different program's new notes are skipped, with a Log warning, while that channel still has held or sustained notes; play them again after release, or choose another channel. The app cannot give one physical channel two independent programs.
5. Port names are saved for portability. On another PC, reopen the picker and choose the corresponding port. A missing or ambiguous name leaves the instrument unavailable and reports the reason in Log. Reconnecting restores routing without replaying old notes. Release held keys and play again. If two ports have identical names, give them distinct names in the operating system or MIDI routing software first.

For Qsynth on Linux, configure its soundfont, audio driver and MIDI input in Qsynth, then select its exposed output destination in this app. The port must appear in the picker before it can be selected. Qsynth runs independently; its driver and font setup are not managed by this app. This procedure has not been tested with Qsynth or hardware. See the [official Qsynth documentation](https://qsynth.sourceforge.io/) for its configuration.

Future events remain in the engine's cancellable queue until due, then use Web MIDI timestamps. This avoids stale future notes after stop, seek or a routing reset on runtimes without `MIDIOutput.clear()`. Dispatch is subject to renderer timer jitter; it is not a measured hardware latency guarantee. `audio.compensationMs.midi` in the computer's settings adjusts sequenced timing only, as described in [FluidSynth and timing offsets](fluidsynth.md). The backend reports no estimated receiver latency.

Simulated-port tests cover selection, bank/program messages, notes, CC, bend, channel/poly pressure, shared pitches/pedal ownership, conflicting program protection, scheduled cancellation, disconnect/reconnect, live and sequenced routing, audition and saved YAML. Physical hardware, Qsynth, operating-system virtual-port setup and Windows operation remain untested.
